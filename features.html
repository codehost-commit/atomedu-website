Features

One tool. Four ways to work.

Proof Lab combines a visual math editor with a rule-based engine. Solve a supported problem, check your own reasoning, generate practice, or explore the graph.

Solve

Enter an expression, equation, or supported structured problem. See the result, the steps used to reach it, and any domain restrictions. The engine reports when a problem is outside its verified coverage.

Check work

Write your own steps. Proof Lab checks supported transitions and points to the first problem it can identify. If you make a valid leap, translucent Ghost steps can show the reasoning between your checkpoints.

Generate

Choose a supported topic and a difficulty from 0.000 to 1.000. Generate a repeatable problem with a seed, try it yourself, then reveal its solution or send it into Check work.

Graph

Plot multiple expressions, pan and zoom, adjust parameters, and inspect curves. This is numerical exploration: the graph does not replace the proof or certify a result.

Visual math input

Build fractions, roots, powers, matrices, and piecewise expressions using the math pad. Fill the gray placeholders, or edit the synchronized LaTeX source directly. Outputs are typeset as math too.

What verification means

The proof has to survive the rules

Proof Lab uses its own math engine to parse, solve, and check supported problems. A valid LaTeX expression only means the notation makes sense; mathematical verification is a separate step. Some results are exact and certified, while unsupported or numerical-only cases are labeled as such.

No account or progress history is required. Your work stays in the current browser session unless you choose to copy or save it.

Current coverage

Algebra and geometry, with clear boundaries

Proof Lab covers selected Algebra 1, Algebra 2, and geometry rule families. It handles many equations, inequalities, functions, sequences, matrices, conics, coordinate problems, measurements, and explicit-premise proofs. It does not claim every problem in a full curriculum, and calculus notation is not a supported solver feature.
